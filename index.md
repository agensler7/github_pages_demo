# Lab 10 GitHub Basics

Problem: What is the correlation between the development of public housing and public transit in the Bay Area, specifically BART stations? Are public housing units built near the popular BART stations? If public housing units are not being built near the most popular BART stations, are they being built near the most popular home origin stations?

Further Definitions & Scope: For our proposal, popular BART stations are classified as the 15 out of 50 BART stations with the highest average weekday riderships, based on the August 2026 monthly ridership report. From August’s report, the top three most popular BART stations are: Embarcadero, Montgomery, and Powell St. According to the 2024 BART Station Profile study, a “home origin” station is when a rider is “coming from home.” We will define popular “home origin” stations as ones with the highest proportions of riders beginning their trip from a BART station near their home. We will be using the BART’s 2016 Transit Oriented Development Guidelines to define “near” a BART station as living within 0.5 miles to the BART. 

Time Frame: 10 years (2016 to 2026). This is because BART began its Transit Oriented Development Policy in 2016.

Geographic Region: Areas near (within 0.5 miles) of the BART lines in the Bay Area. 

Target Population: The target population is individuals who are living in public housing and could benefit from public transport. More specifically, the target population is individuals living in public housing who have commutes to work that could be talent through the BART. 

Intended Outcome: From the BART’s 2016 Customer Satisfaction Survey, 71% of weekday BART trips are commute trips. Public housing units serve as an equalizer and opportunity for equality, and with BART’s great importance for work commuting, it's important to identify if the affordable housing units are providing mobility for individuals who need them.
